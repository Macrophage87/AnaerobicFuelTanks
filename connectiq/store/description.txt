Dual-Tank Anaerobic — see your two anaerobic energy systems, live.

Most power models treat your anaerobic capacity ("W-prime") as a single tank. Your body has two, and they behave very differently:

- PCr (phosphocreatine / alactic) — your "punch." Highest power, small capacity, refills fast (seconds) once you ease off.
- GLY (glycolytic / lactic) — your "sustained dig." Larger capacity, lower power, refills slowly (minutes) and only below critical power (CP). (See A NOTE ON THE TANKS.)

Dual-Tank Anaerobic splits your anaerobic reserve into these two tanks and drives them entirely from your power meter, so at a glance you can see WHICH system you're spending and HOW FAST each will come back.

HOW IT READS
Two bars, one per system:
- Dull color = full or recovering
- Bright color = draining right now (shows live watts)
- Solid red + flash = tank spent
PCr is purple, GLY is green.

The layout adapts to your data-field cell:
- Very wide: two bars side by side
- Wide / short: two bars stacked
- Full screen: tanks on top, plus a summary panel with kJ spent per system and a live Fatigue level
- Tall 1x2 cell: two vertical tanks side by side
Reads clearly on light and dark themes.

RECORDED TO YOUR RIDE
Once CP and W-prime are set, it writes both reserves to the ride's FIT file every second, in joules, as developer fields PCr_J and GLY_J. The field records nothing else. The "Record reserves to FIT" setting (on by default) controls this; a change takes effect the next time the field loads, so start a new ride. Turned off, the field creates no FIT fields.

THE MODEL
Demand above your aerobic supply is met by both systems in parallel: PCr is the immediate buffer and glycolysis ramps in over the first few seconds, so both drain together on a hard effort rather than one after the other. Recovery is system-specific: PCr recovers fast but is efficiency-limited and slows as you fatigue; glycolytic recovers slowly and only below CP, faster the easier you ride. Realism terms (0 turns each off): an aerobic on-ramp for the oxygen deficit at the start of efforts, and fatigue-slowed PCr recovery across repeated bouts. Pauses are handled correctly — the tanks keep refilling for the whole break.

A NOTE ON THE TANKS
"Tank" is a simplified picture for a quick glance, not a literal reservoir. PCr really is a small store that depletes. The glycolytic "tank" is better read as how much metabolic-byproduct buildup (phosphate, H+, etc.) you can still tolerate - NOT running low on carbohydrate (you have plenty on this timescale; lactate is a fuel and a marker, not the cause). That is why glycolytic recovers slowly and only when you ease off, while PCr snaps back fast. Fatigue is multifactorial; the model lumps it into one number for usability.

SET IT UP
Twelve settings drive the model (CP, W-prime, PCr share and max power, recovery time constants, LT1 fraction, efficiency, fatigue, aerobic onset, glycolytic activation). Two ways to get your numbers:
- Work with an LLM: share your ride FIT files and it estimates all twelve, tells you which your data actually constrains, and refines them as you log more workouts. The project README has a ready-to-paste context block.
- Or use the free companion R Shiny calibration app for initial values - it reads your FIT files, flags weakly-constrained parameters, and exports the settings.
CP and W-prime have no default: until both are set the field shows SET CP/W'. The other defaults are sensible; personalize for best accuracy.

WHO IT'S FOR
Racers and interval riders who attack, recover, and repeat — criteriums, cyclocross, punchy road races, hard group rides — where knowing whether you have one more surge in you actually matters.

Note: tank levels are a pacing model, not a measurement of muscle chemistry. Calibrate to your own testing and treat them as a guide. Requires a cycling power meter.
